Music Technology Reading Group Symposium 2026
======

24-25 September  
KTH Royal Institute of Technology

Members of the Music Technology Reading Group (MTRG) are meeting up in Stockholm in September!

The symposium marks the 20th meeting of MTRG, and we celebrate this with a hybrid event hosted by KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden. Researchers from the group will meet and discuss their research, collaborate in a writing workshop, and plan the future developments of MTRG.

We are honoured to invite Associate Professor in Musicology Anders Reuter (Lund University) to deliver a keynote at the symposium. Reuter’s research sits across musicology and digital culture studies and is situated within the growing field of global pop music studies. The keynote will be hosted in collaboration with the Slovenian Musicological Society and will inaugurate their lecture cycle on the intersection of computation and music research. 

Anders Reuter Keynote
------
**The New Forms of Popular Music: Rethinking Musical Form and Listening in Platform Culture**

The twenty-first century has seen the emergence of fundamentally new forms of music. Popular music is arguably at the forefront of this development, with profound transformations spanning production, dissemination, sound, modes of listening, and, ultimately, what even constitutes a musical object. These developments are deeply intertwined: digital programming, streaming, algorithmic distribution, and short-video platforms converge to produce a novel kind of musical material – modular, crossmedial, and textural, and embedded in environments structured by shifting forms of attention.
 
Yet popular music studies remains strongly oriented towards familiar interpretive categories. These include the artist and their biography, text, and meaning. This focus shapes not only how popular music is understood, but also what becomes an object of research, leaving important developments under-researched. At the same time (and with some notable exceptions), the field has neglected the development of new forms of musical analysis. As a result, we know surprisingly little about how contemporary popular music actually sounds or how we listen to it at a time when both are arguably undergoing fundamental change.
 
Drawing on my recent research, this lecture takes the sounding and mediated materiality of contemporary pop as its starting point, bringing musical analysis into dialogue with media philosophy, platform studies, and attention studies. Through analyses of form, timbre, texture, modularity, crossmediality, intimacy, and narrativity, I explore entropy and topology as new ways of conceptualising musical form, and hyperlistening as a corresponding mode of listening. Together, these help theorise the changing relationship between contemporary popular music and the technological, medial, and attentional conditions in which it exists. The larger question is how we might need to update not only how we study contemporary popular music, but what we understand listening – and music – to be.
 
**Bio:**
Anders Reuter is Senior Lecturer in Musicology at Lund University. His research sits at the intersection of popular music studies and digital culture, with a particular interest in how media conditions shape contemporary pop music and listening. His recent work addresses digital materiality, musical form, platformization, attention, intimacy, and K-pop. Before entering academia, Reuter worked professionally as a music producer and journalist.
